# M5 solver and initialization comparisons

M5 is a schema `5.0` experiment family for comparing bounded TRF and LM solvers, two shared-height coordinates (`h` and `q`), and three initializers. It keeps the M4 experiment inputs, unsquared range objective, motion and height priors, geometry evaluation, and camera/playback display. M5 runs and solver sweeps have independent schema and persistence routes; they do not change M1–M4 run schemas. M5.1–M5.4 passed within the recorded numerical, benchmark, persistence, and desktop UI scope. M5.5's implementation decision is to defer the second backend.

## Objective and solver controls

The measurement residual is the original unsquared range difference divided by the assumed range standard deviation. Motion and height-prior residuals remain separate terms in the same least-squares vector and are reported as separate objectives. The optimization uses the analytic M4 physical Jacobian with the exact chain rule for the selected M5 coordinate. The range objective is shared across TRF, LM, `h`, and `q`; `q` does not square the ranges or change the statistical objective.

`solver` is `trf` or `lm`. TRF uses SciPy's native closed box bounds and native bound-constrained projection. LM uses exponential maps for finite one-sided bounds: a lower-bounded coordinate is `lower + exp(u)` and an upper-bounded coordinate is `upper - exp(u)`. LM starts and remains strictly inside those bounds; it can approach a bound asymptotically but cannot attain it. An initial value on a bound is rejected. Exponential underflow, boundary rounding, or a zero transform derivative is retained as an explicit failed start. SciPy LM also requires at least as many residuals as parameters.

An independent reconstruction of the former exponential map found that underflow or boundary rounding could land exactly on a bound with a zero derivative, violating the strict-interior contract. The current revision 2 explicitly rejects lower/upper bound starts, underflow, boundary roundoff, and overflow. It preserves two historical revision 1 domain-invalid results in the earlier independent-review artifact; the current acceptance bundle uses revision 2.

## Shared-height coordinates

The height parameterization applies where the model has one shared relative-height degree of freedom. Let `h` be the positive beacon-minus-tag (or tag-below-surveyed-plane) separation from the M4 datum. `h` is measured in metres. M5 selects the positive relative-height branch for both `h` and `q`; this branch choice is a convention and does not establish global uniqueness. Elsewhere `h` retains the signed physical coordinate.

`q = h²` is measured in square metres and is constrained to be at least `FLOOR²`, with `FLOOR = 1e-6 m`. The inverse map uses `h = sqrt(q)` on the selected positive branch. `q` is available only for joint planar fixed/shared tag heights with shared beacon height, or for shared tag height under a horizontal known beacon plane. It is rejected for models that do not have one supported shared relative height. This restriction also avoids treating `q` as an ordinary coordinate for independently varying world-z maps.

For a range `R = sqrt(horizontal_distance² + q)`, the normalized measurement residual derivative is `d(R/σ)/dq = 1/(2 R σ)`. The implementation evaluates this directly; it does not form an intermediate `1/sqrt(q)`, including at the limiting coordinate. In `h`, the corresponding derivative includes the canonical frame sign. Shared constant height cancels from the time-weighted motion prior, so its motion-prior Jacobian column is zero. Height and motion priors continue to be evaluated in physical model coordinates.

M5 records both the physical geometry Jacobian and the native `h`/`q` Jacobian. The native rank calculation scales metre coordinates by the median range and the `q` coordinate by its square. Thus the `q` coordinate uses `m²`, and its rank/conditioning describes local sensitivity in that coordinate; it is not extra range information. At `q = 0`, the inverse map to `h` is singular even though the range derivative with respect to `q` can be finite. Rank remains a local diagnostic and does not prove global uniqueness.

When paired starts are transferred between compatible M5 variants, the source's canonical physical truth/geometry is projected into the target model's native coordinates, bounds, and selected positive shared-height branch. Both `h` and `q` impose the positive branch. The saved projection displacement measures the difference caused by this model convention; it is not an optimizer lower bound or an irreducible range error. For example, a retained negative-height fixture with truth at `z = -3 m` has an M5 native map projection error of `3.000002 m` in both `h` and `q`, while the preceding M4 projection error is `0 m`. The reflected positive-branch geometry nevertheless reproduces ranges to `1.8e-15 m`. These results are in `evidence/m5/extra_runs/negative_shared_height_branch_2a247bbb.json` and the independent review. They demonstrate the branch convention and reflection ambiguity, not a failure of range recovery.

## Initializers

`initialization` is `independent`, `truth_near`, or `algebraic`.

- `independent` starts are generated from estimator inputs and the M4 physical model. `initialization_error_m` adds Gaussian perturbations, in metres, to canonical physical M4 parameters before the `h`/`q` mapping; generated starts are projected into model bounds. The UI defaults this scale to zero. Compatible starts supplied by a paired sweep are reused without adding another perturbation.
- `truth_near` is a diagnostic initializer that requires explicitly supplied truth-derived geometry. It is not a measurement-only initialization claim. Pairing is tracked separately for each initializer group because different initializer methods intentionally produce different starts.
- `algebraic` is a measurement-only proposal for synchronized, complete epochs with one positive range per beacon, 3–8 beacons, at least six informative epochs, and the joint planar fixed/shared-tag plus shared-beacon-height model. It fits a scaled, lifted squared-range linear system to propose physical geometry. The proposal is not the final objective: the estimator still optimizes unsquared range residuals. It selects `d > 0`, beacon 2 with positive y, and positive height separation to choose a representative reflection branch. These conventions do not prove uniqueness. Rank loss, unsupported timing/model inputs, nonphysical coefficients, failed closure checks, and data outside the disclosed physical tolerance return invalid/unsupported diagnostics. M5 retains the failed initializer attempt and does not silently fall back to another method.

The initializer status is saved in each start's diagnostics (`algebraic_initializer.status` or `initializer.status`) and in each sweep start-table row. `success` means the initializer produced a valid proposal; `invalid`, `failed`, and `unsupported` remain separate visible outcomes. Optimizer convergence, residual fit, and geometric accuracy are separate measurements.

## Paired sweeps and persistence

The sweep factors are `initialization_error_m`, `noise_std_m`, `beacon_count`, `vertical_separation`, and `coverage`. Each factor value and measurement seed produces one experiment. Variants are explicitly labeled and may compare solver, height parameterization, or initializer options. Within a value/seed/initializer group, the first variant supplies physical starts; later compatible variants receive native model-projected versions of those starts. The projection is recorded as a projection outcome, separate from truth error. Different initialization groups deliberately use different starts, so such a comparison is not a solver-only comparison. Sweeps retain every run and every attempted start, including invalid initializers and failures.

Limits are 1–10 distinct values, 1–10 distinct unsigned 32-bit measurement seeds, 1–4 variants, at most 40 runs, at most 12 starts per run, at most 240 total starts, and at most 5,000 function evaluations per start. Variant controls cannot silently change shared start count, seed, initialization error, physical height/map model, priors, or timing choices. The initializer method can differ explicitly. Layout factors require a generated beacon map; explicit coordinates are not silently resized.

The version `5.0`, `solver_sweep` artifact retains normalized config, pairing references, source runs, the all-attempt `start_table`, aggregates, and thresholds. Failures stay in all-start denominators. Aggregate position, map, height, range, objective, and runtime distributions distinguish all attempts from selected starts. The acceptance thresholds copied from M4 are exact-data maximum geometry error `1e-5 m`, noisy geometry error `0.25 m`, exact-data maximum range residual `1e-7 m`, and noisy normalized residual RMS `2`; those thresholds are independent of optimizer convergence.

The local API is asynchronous. `POST /api/m5/runs` accepts `{ "config": ..., "options": ... }` and returns a job id; `/api/jobs/<id>` reports progress and the generic `/api/runs` endpoints list and reopen retained runs. `POST /api/m5/sweeps` accepts `base_experiment`, `options`, `factor`, `values`, `seeds`, and labeled `variants`; `GET /api/m5/sweeps` and `GET /api/m5/sweeps/<id>` list and reopen the saved sweep, with `/csv` for the retained attempt table. `POST /api/m5/import` validates and saves a run or `solver_sweep` artifact without solving it. M5 runs can use the shared run-comparison API and UI.

## Recorded numerical evidence

The current acceptance evidence is `evidence/m5/acceptance_20261014_124422_c74e2389`. Its nine sweeps contain 160 runs and 320 starts; eight additional service fixtures contain 16 starts, for 336 recorded attempts overall. In the sweeps, 51 starts failed, including 40 invalid algebraic-initializer proposals, and one optimizer start was nonconverged. The other 268 sweep starts converged. Four forced-nonconvergence fixture starts (two per solver) are separately retained. Every artifact was reopened and validated offline, and the bundle reports that 649 preservation checks found no changes.

The independent mathematical review `evidence/m5/independent_math_review.json` passed all seven checks: distance-Jacobian scaling; supported `h` model Jacobians; algebraic physics support; LM domains and failure retention; truth independence and pairing; native projection and failed seeds; and retained acceptance artifacts. The review independently assembled distance residuals, analytic derivatives, and lifted physical coefficients rather than using simulator residuals/Jacobians as expected values. Its numerical checks confirm the positive relative-height branch convention and the coordinate-dependent interpretation of rank. Review evidence does not establish global uniqueness.

In exact-data independent-basin fixtures, the median position errors across `trf/h`, `trf/q`, `lm/h`, and `lm/q` are on the order of `1e-15 m`; these are fixture results, not general accuracy guarantees. In weak-geometry cells, median position errors reach about `5.9–7.1 m` with median range errors near `1 mm` in one cell. Low range residual therefore does not establish correct geometry. Invalid algebraic proposals and failed/nonconverged estimates remain visible in the all-attempt totals.

The run-level suite `evidence/m5/test_results_delivery.txt` records 127 passing tests in 10.119 seconds. The benchmark attempt in `evidence/m5/benchmark_attempt2.txt` points to `evidence/m5/benchmark_20261014_123003_fc71f42c/performance_benchmark.json`: 48 paired records compare SciPy TRF/LM and `h`/`q` on the same noisy observations and physical starts across 4/8 beacons and 12/24 samples, with three repeats per case. The wrapper timing includes fitting, diagnostics, and validation, but excludes JSON disk saving. All recorded starts succeeded. These small local timings are descriptive and do not demonstrate a general performance advantage. The separate history benchmark reports warm listing times of 0.082 s and 0.045 s for 564 records, with no unreadable records; summaries use a file-metadata cache while detail and import still validate full contents.

## Implementation decision and limits

M5 retains SciPy TRF and LM. The decision record `evidence/m5/second_implementation_decision.json` defers Ceres because no unmet numerical operation or measured larger-problem need currently justifies a native port; it explicitly makes no claim about Ceres performance or cross-implementation agreement. Geomstats is also deferred: the reviewed Euclidean proper-rigid alignment is sufficiently handled by NumPy SVD, with no measured benefit supporting another dependency. Revisit Ceres if identical-data comparisons expose a discrepancy or a larger workload shows a measured performance need.

M5 acceptance passed for its defined scope. The backend audit and UI record document lifecycle and visual evidence, along with explicit checkpoint/write-failure and mobile-layout limits. A 390×844 check confirmed the controls fit and run/sweep history scroll, while the full page still has horizontal overflow. See [M5 acceptance](M5_ACCEPTANCE.md) for the evidence and limits.
